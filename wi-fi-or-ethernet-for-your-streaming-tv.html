Wi-Fi or Ethernet for Your Streaming TV

A wired connection is the single most reliable fix for live TV that buffers, but running a cable across the house is not always practical. Knowing when it really matters saves a lot of effort.

If the TV sits in the same room as the router, or one wall away, a good 5 GHz Wi-Fi link is usually enough for HD and even 4K streams. Problems start when two or more walls, a floor or a kitchen stand between the router and the TV.

In that case, Ethernet removes the guesswork. A flat cable along the baseboard costs a few dollars, and many streaming sticks accept a USB Ethernet adapter even though they have no port of their own.

When a cable is out of the question, powerline adapters carry the network over the electrical wiring and often beat Wi-Fi through thick walls. A mesh system is the next option for large homes, as long as one node sits close to the TV.

Whatever you choose, test it with a long live event rather than a quick speed test; steady delivery over an hour matters more than a big number for ten seconds. More setup tips and plan comparisons are on Best IPTV for USA.
